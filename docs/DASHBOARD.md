# The dashboard

The short version is in the [README](../README.md#the-dashboard). This page has every key, the jump backends, the mouse and the bell. `docs/KEYS.md` is the design note behind the keys, and `?` in the dashboard lists them.

`sitrep dash` is a full-screen view with three tabs and a status bar, written with Bubble Tea and Lip Gloss and built into the same program. `sitrep dash --demo` shows made-up sessions, read-only, and reads nothing from disk, so you can try the tabs, the keys and the mouse before installing. Without a terminal (a pipe, `TERM` unset or `dumb`) it prints one frame and exits, and `sitrep status` always does: `sitrep status [--tab cards|table|wall] [--width n] [-a]`.

The three tabs are:

1. **Cards** shows Objective, Progress, Now and the queue of each session, wrapped with a hanging indent. The labels, the numbers of the queue items and the session's name are bold (the labels and numbers also in the accent colour), so that they stand out from the text. A session that needs you, or was recently active, has a bar down the left edge of its card in the colour of its state, and the state of one that needs you is bold in that colour. The card has no background colour, and three columns in front of it: the bar, the selection (an arrow on the first line and a rail down the rest) and a space. A screen with many sessions cuts each field to three lines and each queue item to two, and when even that does not fit five or more sessions (the list scrolls instead for fewer), each session shows only its header and its Now line and the inbox shrinks to `Inbox: N items (i)`; when the sessions fit with room to spare, the text is shown in more lines (up to eight), so a screen with two sessions is not left half empty.
2. **Table** is a summary line (`3 running: 1 working, 1 waiting, 1 idle?, 2 ended`) and one line per session, with its state, queue size, the share of its context window left (CTX) and what it is doing now. CTX is `62%`, drawn as `!9%` at 10% or less (and bold at 20% or less) so the plain text carries the warning, and `-` where the session has not said (a background session with no status line, or one that has not drawn it yet under 0.8). It is dropped on a narrow screen before the text of NOW is cut. The numbers come from the status line: Claude Code gives `sitrep statusline` the window size and what is in it, and it records them in `sessions/<id>/context`.
3. **Wall** lays the sessions out as tiles of one height, in as many columns as the width holds (the width divided by 30), so that many sessions fit on one screen. The sessions that need you come first, then the order of the other tabs, and the rows of tiles scroll, with `+N above` and `+N more` counting tiles. A tile has the label and state, the current activity, the queue (`no queue` when there is none, so the grid stays aligned) and the directory with the context left. `s` cycles the size: standard (four rows), roomy (six, with Objective and Progress) and compact (two, with the queue as `+N`). On the Wall `h`, `l`, `j`, `k` and the arrows move between tiles and between rows of tiles, `tab` and `shift+tab` switch tabs, and `left` on the first tile and `right` on the last also go to the previous and the next tab, so the arrow keys that walk you in walk you out. `--tab wall` opens on it, and `sitrep status --tab wall` prints one frame. See [WALL.md](WALL.md).

A session that waits for you, meaning Claude has stopped and the next move is yours, is drawn with a soft pink-red bar down its card (a background on the whole row in the Table and the Wall) and a `●` before its state, until you send it a prompt. A session stopped at a permission dialog counts too, and its state reads `blocked`, until you answer. The status bar then leads with `● 2 need you`. The rule is the `needs_input` field of `sitrep ls --json`.

Keys:

1. `up`, `down`, `j` and `k` move the selection, which follows its session through refreshes and re-sorts, `gg` and `G` (and `home` and `end`) select the first and last, and `ctrl+d`, `ctrl+u`, `pgdn` and `pgup` move by a page. A lone `g` waits for a second one, shown as `g-` in the status bar; any other key cancels it, and does nothing else, as in vim.
2. `enter` jumps to the terminal that shows the selected session, when the dashboard itself runs inside tmux, cmux, iTerm2 or Terminal.app. It finds it through the session's process id, and through the `claude attach` client for a session that runs in the background. In tmux it selects the window, pane and client by tmux's own ids. In cmux it reads `cmux top --all --processes` to find the surface that holds the process, and focuses its window, workspace, pane and surface. In iTerm2 and Terminal.app it asks `osascript` which window and tab has the tty of the session's process (or of the `claude attach` client), and selects it; the first time, macOS asks whether the terminal may control the application, and if you say no, the jump says where to allow it (System Settings > Privacy & Security > Automation). A pane inside tmux wins over the application around it, so the order is tmux, cmux, iTerm2, Terminal. The session may run in a different terminal than the dashboard: on macOS both applications are always asked (each only when it is running), so a dashboard in cmux can jump to a session in iTerm2 or Terminal.app. The other way does not work by design: cmux only lets processes started inside it connect, so a dashboard in iTerm2 or Terminal.app cannot jump to a session in cmux, and the status bar says what cmux answered. If it cannot find one it says so and changes nothing. `--quit-on-jump` leaves the dashboard after a good jump, for a tmux popup.
3. `v` (view) or the space bar opens the selected session in full, with nothing cut off, its whole queue, and how full its context window is (`375k / 1M tokens  (37.5% used, 62.5% left)  seen 3m ago`, where seen is when its status line last drew). `q`, `esc` or `v` closes it, `up` and `down` show the neighbouring sessions, and `pgup`, `pgdn`, `ctrl+d`, `ctrl+u`, the space bar and `b` scroll, with `gg` and `G` at the top and the bottom. `e` and `i` open the session's queue and the inbox from there, and `q` out of the queue comes back to the detail view.
4. `tab`, `shift+tab`, `h`, `l`, `left` and `right` switch tabs (on the Wall `h`, `l` and the arrows move between tiles, and only `tab` and `shift+tab` switch), a number from `1` to `9` asks whether to move that item of the inbox (the numbers the Cards tab lists) to the end of the selected session's queue (`y` does it, `n` or `esc` cancels; it is `t` of the inbox screen, with the same checks and the same guard on the item's text), `a` shows or hides ended sessions, and `s` cycles the sort of the Cards or the Table (the Wall's `s` is the tile size). The sorts are activity, state, directory, queue size and name, which lists the sessions you named (`sitrep alias`) first, by name, and then the others by directory. Sessions that tie go on to the next key: the directory sort by name, the name sort and the queue sort by directory, and what is still tied stays in activity order. The Table starts on activity and the Cards on state, which puts the sessions that need you first, and both go on through the others. The Table always shows its sort, and the Cards say `sort: activity` and so on when it is not the default. Activity is when a session last did something: its state changed, or its transcript was written, whichever is later, so a session in one long turn is not below sessions that stopped long ago.
5. `/` filters the sessions by a case-insensitive match in their id, directory, state, Objective, Progress and Now. What you type is text, `enter` keeps the filter and `esc` clears it.
6. `y` copies the selected session's id and `Y` its `claude --resume <id>` command, through the terminal's clipboard escape (OSC 52); the status bar shows the text as well. An id with unusual characters is never copied.
7. `i` opens the inbox and `e` the selected session's queue, as one list. Its numbers are bold in the accent colour, and an item that takes more than one line is followed by an empty line, so that where one item ends and the next begins can be seen. In a queue, `o` (or `a`) types a new item, `enter` (or `E`) edits the selected one in place (the line starts with its text, and `esc` cancels), `dd` (vi's delete line; `x` and the delete key do the same) removes the selected one after a `y` or `n`, and a waiting `d` shows as `d-`, `K` and `J` move it up and down, `T` moves it to the front, `m` sends it to another queue (a short list opens with the inbox and the running sessions, `j` and `k` choose, `enter` sends it to the end of that queue and `esc` cancels; it is `sitrep move`, with the same guard on the item's text), `P` types the Objective to pin and `U` unpins it. In the inbox, `t` takes the selected item into the session that was selected when you pressed `i`, after a `y` or `n`. The keys that act on an item pass its text, so they refuse, and say so, if the queue changed since the list was drawn.
8. `n` names the selected session: a line opens in the status bar with its current name, `enter` sets it, an empty line clears it, and `esc` cancels. It runs `sitrep alias`, so the same rules apply, and an ended session can be named too.
9. `z` snoozes the selected session: it is drawn with a hollow `○` and no colour, and drops out of the count of those that need you, until it changes by itself (the next prompt, or the end of the next turn). `z` again ends it at once.
10. `p` prunes the sessions that are gone: it shows what `sitrep prune --dry-run` would do and asks. `y` moves their queue items to the inbox, `n` leaves the items, and `esc` cancels. It will not run without the live-session registry.
11. `r` refreshes, and `?` lists the keys and colours from every screen (any key closes the list, and the screen under it is as it was). `q` goes back from the detail view, the queue and the prune screen, and quits from the tabs; `ctrl+c` quits from anywhere.

The terminal bell rings once when a session starts needing you. It stays quiet on the first load and for a snoozed session. `--no-bell` or `SITREP_TUI_BELL=0` turns it off. `--read-only` or `SITREP_TUI_READONLY` turns off every key that changes state. `--interval` sets the refresh (default 3s), and `--tab table` opens on the Table tab. `--mouse` or `SITREP_TUI_MOUSE=1` makes a click select a row or a tile, a click on the name of a tab open that tab, and a double click jump, as `enter` does; it is off by default, because the terminal then selects text in the dashboard only with Shift (Option in iTerm2 and Terminal.app) held.

The dashboard reads the same snapshot as `sitrep ls --json`, and changes state only by running the same fixed commands you can run yourself (`prune`, `snooze` and the queue edits), in the same process, after a preview or a confirm. If a refresh fails, the last good screen stays and the status bar says why. It strips control and format characters from everything it draws.

## Colours

`sitrep colors` is a full-screen tool for the dashboard's colours, and the `C` key of `sitrep dash` opens the same tool over your real sessions (`q` returns to the dashboard, which then refreshes, so a colour you wrote shows at once; in a read-only dashboard the tool is read-only, and `dash --demo` has no colour tool, since it reads no file). The top of the screen is a preview, drawn by the dashboard's own code on made-up sessions, with a row of each kind: needs you, faded, recent activity, snoozed, working, idle, plus the tabs, the status bar, dim text and an error. Below it is the list of settings: the theme, then `accent`, `dim`, `hot`, `attn`, `error` and `snoozed`.

`j` and `k` move. `enter` on a colour opens a field. Type `#rrggbb` or a terminal colour number from 0 to 255, or step through the colours: `h` and `l` (or left and right) turn the hue, `j` and `k` (or down and up) change the lightness, and `H` and `L` the chroma. The steps use the OKLCH colour model, in which turning the hue keeps the lightness, so the text sitrep picks for a background stays readable; a colour that is not on the screen's range loses chroma, never hue or lightness. On a terminal colour number, `up` and `down` walk the 256 numbers instead. On the theme row `enter` opens the gallery: every theme in its own colours, one to a line, with the one in use marked ✓ (`j` and `k` move, `enter` chooses, `t` switches between a dark and a light terminal, `esc` goes back), and `h` and `l` move to the next or the previous theme without it. `r` puts a setting back to the theme's own, and `t` switches the preview between a dark and a light terminal; a colour has one value for each, and editing one side keeps the other. A `*` marks a change that is not written yet, and `set` marks a colour that `sitrep.conf` holds; a colour with no mark is the theme's own. `--light` and `--dark` fix the kind of terminal, which is otherwise asked of the terminal.

`w` shows the old and the new `SITREP_THEME` and `SITREP_COLORS` lines and asks. On `y` it saves a copy of `sitrep.conf` as `sitrep.conf.bak.<time>` in the state folder (before every write, so the copies accumulate there), then changes those two lines and nothing else. A `sitrep.conf` that is a link is written through, so a dotfiles link stays a link, and when the file changed meanwhile nothing is written. A running `sitrep dash` shows the change at its next refresh. The environment wins over the file, so the tool warns when `SITREP_THEME` or `SITREP_COLORS` is set there. `--read-only` (or `SITREP_TUI_READONLY`) turns `w` off.
